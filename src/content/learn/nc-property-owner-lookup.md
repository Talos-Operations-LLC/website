## Step 1: How do you find the county tax card?

The tax card (sometimes called the property record card) is the county tax office's record for one parcel. Most counties put it online through a tax or GIS search where you can enter the property address. Where a county doesn't, the same record is available at the tax office.

Parcels are identified by a PIN, a parcel identification number, and some counties also use their own account number, often called a REID (real estate ID). Once you have either, it's the most reliable way back to the same parcel, since addresses get abbreviated and misspelled.

The mailing address and the property address are separate fields. The mailing address is where the county sends the tax bill, and it can be a different house, a PO box or an out-of-state address.

## Step 2: How do you confirm the owner against the recorded deed?

The deed is the recorded source document, kept by the county Register of Deeds. Many tax cards list the deed's book and page, which takes you straight to it. Otherwise, search the Register of Deeds grantee index for the owner name on the tax card.

On the deed, check three things:

- **Grantee name**: it matches the owner on the tax card
- **Recording date**: it's the most recent deed for the parcel
- **Property description**: the lot, subdivision or parcel reference matches the tax card

To check that nothing has been recorded since, search the owner's name in the grantor index from that recording date forward. [Grantor vs. grantee](/learn/grantor-vs-grantee/) explains how the two sides of the index work.

## Why can the two records disagree?

The tax card is updated after the deed, by a different office, so the two can disagree right after a sale. They also differ over name formatting, multiple owners, and mailing-address changes that never reach the county. [Deed vs. tax card](/learn/deed-vs-tax-card/) covers each of these, and [why county records lag behind sales](/learn/county-record-lag/) covers the timing.

If the deed names an LLC rather than a person, the trail continues at the NC Secretary of State. [Finding the owner behind an LLC](/learn/llc-property-owner-nc/) walks through it.
