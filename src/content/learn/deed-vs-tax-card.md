## What is each record, and which office keeps it?

The deed is the document that transfers ownership. After closing it is recorded at the county Register of Deeds, which indexes it under the names of the grantor and grantee and assigns it a book and page. The recorded deed is the source document for who took title and when.

The tax card is the county tax office's record for a parcel, kept for listing and taxing property. It carries the owner's name, a mailing address, the property address, the parcel number and the assessed value. The tax office updates the owner after a deed is recorded: the tax card follows the deed rather than creating ownership.

## Why do they disagree right after a sale?

The deed is recorded first. The tax office processes the transfer afterward, in a separate system, so the tax card can still show the previous owner after the new deed is recorded. [Why county records lag behind sales](/learn/county-record-lag/) covers that window.

## How do name formats make the same owner look different?

- **Name order.** Deed indexes list people last name first, and tax cards can too, while the deed itself is written in natural order.
- **Two owners.** Two owners can appear as separate rows in the deed index and as one line joined with "&" on the tax card.
- **Trusts.** A property held in trust may be listed under the trust's name, the trustee's name, or both.
- **Entities.** Punctuation differs between records: periods in initials, commas before "INC".

## Why is the mailing address a separate question?

The tax card's mailing address is where the county sends the tax bill. It is a separate field from the property address, and it isn't part of the deed's conveyance. Owners who move, rent the property out or sell may never update it with the county. [Verifying a mailing address](/learn/verify-mailing-address/) describes how it's checked.

## How do you check both, and what does a match look like?

1. Pull the tax card for the property address and note the owner, mailing address and parcel number.
2. Find the most recent deed for the parcel, from the book and page on the tax card or by searching the grantee index for the owner's name.
3. Compare the deed's grantee name with the tax card owner, allowing for the format differences above.
4. Compare the deed's property description with the tax card's parcel reference.
5. Check the deed's recording date against the sale date on the tax card, if the card shows one.

A match means the grantee on the most recent deed is the owner on the tax card, allowing for formatting, and the parcel references agree.
