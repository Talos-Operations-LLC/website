## What do grantor and grantee mean, in a plain example?

Say Dana Whitfield sells a house to Marcus and Ellen Ortiz. The deed names Dana Whitfield as the grantor and Marcus Ortiz and Ellen Ortiz as the grantees. Years later, when the Ortizes sell, they are the grantors on that next deed, and the buyer is the grantee.

The same two roles appear on documents other than deeds. On a deed of trust, the borrower is the grantor and the trustee is the grantee, and county indexes often list the lender (the beneficiary) on the grantee side too, so both can appear. That's why an owner's name in the grantor index can turn up on mortgages as well as sales.

## How is a deed index organized?

Each county's Register of Deeds keeps an index of every recorded document, searchable by grantor name and by grantee name. Most NC counties put the index online, though how far back the online index goes varies by county. A typical entry carries:

- **Names**: the grantor(s) and grantee(s), usually last name first
- **Recording date**: when the Register of Deeds recorded the document
- **Book and page**: where the document sits in the records, and in many counties an instrument number too
- **Instrument type**: deed, deed of trust, satisfaction, easement, and so on
- Sometimes a short property description, such as a lot number or subdivision

## When do you search backward, and when forward?

**Backward** means looking up a current owner's name in the grantee index to find the deed they took title by. That deed's grantor is the previous owner. Repeat with the previous owner's name and you walk the ownership back one deed at a time.

**Forward** means looking up a name in the grantor index, starting from the date that person acquired the property, to see whether they later conveyed it or recorded anything against it. It's how people check whether the owner on an older deed is still the owner today.

Chaining these searches together, deed by deed, is how a chain of title is put together. [How a chain of title is checked in NC](/learn/chain-of-title-nc/) walks through that method.

## What index quirks trip people up?

- **Name order.** Indexes list people last name first, with or without a comma between surname and given names, depending on the county's system.
- **Two owners.** A deed to two owners can be indexed with a separate row for each name, though some indexes put co-owners who share a surname on one row.
- **Trusts.** A deed into a trust may be indexed under the trust's name, the trustee's name or both, so a search on the individual can come up empty.
- **Entities.** Periods in initials and commas before "INC" change how an entity's name reads from one record to the next.
