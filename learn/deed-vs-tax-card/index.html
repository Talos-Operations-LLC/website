What's the difference between the deed and the tax card?

The deed is the recorded source document: it says who took title and when. The tax card is the county tax office's record for the parcel, kept for listing and taxing property.

What is each record, and which office keeps it?

The deed is the document that transfers ownership. After closing it is recorded at the county Register of Deeds, which indexes it under the names of the grantor and grantee and assigns it a book and page. The recorded deed is the source document for who took title and when.

The tax card is the county tax office's record for a parcel, kept for listing and taxing property. It carries the owner's name, a mailing address, the property address, the parcel number and the assessed value.

Why is the mailing address a separate question?

The tax card's mailing address is where the county sends the tax bill. It is a separate field from the property address, and it isn't part of the deed's conveyance. Verifying a mailing address describes how it's checked.

How do you check both, and what does a match look like?

  1. Pull the tax card for the property address and note the owner, mailing address and parcel number.
  2. Find the most recent deed for the parcel, from the book and page on the tax card or by searching the grantee index for the owner's name.
  3. Compare the deed's grantee name with the tax card owner.
  4. Compare the deed's property description with the tax card's parcel reference.
  5. Check the deed's recording date against the sale date on the tax card, if the card shows one.

A match means the grantee on the most recent deed is the owner on the tax card and the parcel references agree.

Frequently asked questions

Which record is "right"?

They answer different questions. The recorded deed is the source document for who took title; the tax card is the tax office's working copy.
