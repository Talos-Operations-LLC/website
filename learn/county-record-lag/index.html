Why do county ownership records lag behind sales?

When a property sells in North Carolina, the deed is recorded and indexed at the Register of Deeds. The county tax office then processes the transfer separately, so the tax card can still show the previous owner after the new deed is recorded. Anyone mailing to the tax-card address during that window may be mailing to someone who has moved.

What path does a deed travel after closing?

  1. Closing. The seller signs the deed to the buyer.
  2. Recording. The deed is presented to the county Register of Deeds, recorded and given a book and page.
  3. Indexing. The deed is entered in the grantor and grantee index, where it becomes searchable by name.
  4. Tax office transfer. The county tax office updates the owner on the tax card, and the mailing address if a new one is supplied.

Where does the lag come from?

Recording and the tax transfer happen in separate offices, in separate systems, each on its own schedule. Until the tax office processes the transfer, the tax card reflects the owner before the sale.

Third-party copies of county data can trail further. A statewide parcel dataset reflects the date it was last refreshed: in one case we checked, it showed no sales after the end of 2025, about eight months behind the county's own records.

What does a "last action" date on a tax card mean?

Some county tax cards print a "last action" date for the record. A sale recorded after that date may not be reflected on the card yet.

How does the lag show up in practice?

  • Mail to the tax-card address comes back, or is forwarded.
  • Notices go out addressed to the previous owner.
  • The new owner doesn't receive mail sent from county records.
  • A sale appears in the deed index but not yet on the tax card.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the lag typically?

It varies by county and over the year, and Fee Simple doesn't publish a figure yet. In one case observed in our runs, a county tax card still showed the previous owner five days after the new deed was recorded. [ELIJAH: observed ranges from the assessor-lag data, labeled as observed]

Does every county lag the same?

Not in what we've observed. Each county's tax office processes transfers in its own system, and the gaps we've seen differ: a tax card still behind five days after recording in one county, and at least seven days in another.

Is the deed index also delayed?

In the cases we've observed, the deed index has been ahead of the tax card, not behind it. It is the record the tax card updates from.
