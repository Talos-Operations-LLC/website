What's the difference between a grantor and a grantee?

The grantor is the party conveying the property; the grantee is the party receiving it. Every North Carolina Register of Deeds indexes recorded documents both ways, so you can search backward (who did this owner buy from?) or forward (did this owner later sell?). Reading the index correctly is the foundation of every ownership check.

What do grantor and grantee mean, in a plain example?

Say Dana Whitfield sells a house to Marcus and Ellen Ortiz. The deed names Dana Whitfield as the grantor and Marcus Ortiz and Ellen Ortiz as the grantees. Years later, when the Ortizes sell, they are the grantors on that next deed, and the buyer is the grantee.

The same two roles appear on documents other than deeds. On a deed of trust, the borrower is the grantor and the trustee is the grantee. That's why an owner's name in the grantor index can turn up on mortgages as well as sales.

How is a deed index organized?

Each county's Register of Deeds keeps an index of every recorded document, searchable by grantor name and by grantee name. A typical entry carries:

  • Names: the grantor(s) and grantee(s)
  • Recording date: when the Register of Deeds recorded the document
  • Book and page: where the document sits in the records
  • Instrument type: deed, deed of trust, satisfaction, easement, and so on
  • Sometimes a short property description, such as a lot number or subdivision
Sample index entry. Names and numbers are invented.

GranteeORTIZ MARCUS J; ORTIZ ELLEN R

When do you search backward, and when forward?

Backward means looking up a current owner's name in the grantee index to find the deed they took title by. That deed's grantor is the previous owner. Repeat with the previous owner's name and you walk the ownership back one deed at a time.

Forward means looking up a name in the grantor index, starting from the date that person acquired the property, to see whether they later conveyed it or recorded anything against it. It's how people check whether the owner on an older deed is still the owner today.

Chaining these searches together, deed by deed, is how a chain of title is put together. How a chain of title is checked in NC walks through that method.

Frequently asked questions

Is the grantor always the seller?

No. The grantor is whichever party conveys the interest, whether or not anything is paid. On a gift deed the grantor receives nothing. On a trustee's deed or an executor's deed the grantor signs in a representative role, for a trust or an estate. On a deed of trust the borrower is the grantor, conveying to a trustee as security for a loan.

What's a book and page number?

It is the location of a recorded document in the Register of Deeds records: the book (volume) it was recorded in and the page it starts on. It is how a recorded document is cited and pulled.
