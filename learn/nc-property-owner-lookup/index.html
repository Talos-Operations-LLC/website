How do you find out who owns a property in North Carolina?

Every North Carolina county keeps ownership records in two offices: the county tax office, whose tax card shows the owner's name and mailing address, and the Register of Deeds, which holds the recorded deed. To look up an owner, pull the tax card for the address, then confirm it against the most recent deed. The same two steps apply in every county, online where the county offers it and at the office where it doesn't.

Step 1: How do you find the county tax card?

The tax card (sometimes called the property record card) is the county tax office's record for one parcel. Search the county's tax or GIS site for the property address, or ask the county tax office.

Parcels are identified by a PIN, a parcel identification number. Wake County also uses its own account number, the REID (real estate ID).

A typical tax card shows:

Sample tax card. Names, addresses and numbers are invented.
OwnerORTIZ MARCUS J; ORTIZ ELLEN R
Mailing addressPO BOX 2210, APEX NC 27502
Property address418 BRIAR CREEK LN
PIN0000-00-0000
Deed book / page4127 / 318
Sale date03/14/2019

The mailing address and the property address are separate fields. The mailing address is where the county sends the tax bill, and it can be a different house, a PO box or an out-of-state address.

Step 2: How do you confirm the owner against the recorded deed?

The deed is the recorded source document, kept by the county Register of Deeds. If the tax card lists the deed's book and page, that takes you straight to it. Otherwise, search the Register of Deeds grantee index for the owner name on the tax card.

On the deed, check three things:

  • Grantee name: it matches the owner on the tax card
  • Recording date: it's the most recent deed for the parcel
  • Property description: the lot, subdivision or parcel reference matches the tax card

To check that nothing has been recorded since, search the owner's name in the grantor index from that recording date forward. Grantor vs. grantee explains how the two sides of the index work.

If the deed names an LLC rather than a person, the trail continues at the NC Secretary of State. Finding the owner behind an LLC walks through it.

Frequently asked questions

What do copies cost?

Certified copies carry a fee set by state statute, and a Register of Deeds may also charge for uncertified copies.

What if the owner is an LLC?

County records stop at the entity's name. The NC Secretary of State's business registry lists the LLC's registered agent and filing history, and matching the deed's grantee name to that filing is how people find who is behind an entity-owned parcel.
